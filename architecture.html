ARCHARCH — Container architecture

One retrofitted shipping container in a dense mixed-wood forest: a light steam/solar-punk one-bedroom stonework cabin-shop, off grid. Swap the interior modules, change the exterior, then join and stack units into something much bigger.

  • Drag the render to orbit. Scroll or use the zoom keys. CUTAWAY removes the roof and the walls facing you so the interior is always visible.
  • Each bay of a container holds one module. Pick a unit, then set its bays — every combination is valid; the manifest below updates as you go.
  • The frame is the frame: nothing here needs a structural change to the box except the buried option, which does (see the notes).

MOD.04.2MODULES

MODULESInterior modules

Every bay takes one of these. Storage runs floor to ceiling, submarine style; the premium grade swaps steel for teak and brass, the utilitarian grade keeps it painted and bolted.

  • BERTHFull berth over drawer storage, lockers above, reading light, curtain rail. The one bedroom.
  • GALLEYCounter, sink, induction hob, fridge drawer, overhead lockers. Compact as a boat galley.
  • WET ROOMShower pan, composting toilet, basin, roof vent. Frosted partition.
  • LOCKER WALLFloor-to-ceiling submarine lockers with recessed latches. Where everything lives.
  • WORKBENCHHardwood bench, vise, pegboard wall, tool shelf, task light. The shop.
  • MICRO-FARMThree-tier hydroponic racks under grow lights. Greens and herbs year-round; a roof light over the bay outside.
  • POWER STATIONBattery rack, inverter, charge controller, breaker panel. Feeds the roof solar and the turbine mast.
  • WATER PLANTTwin tanks, filter stack, pump, copper runs. Fed from the roof gutter and rain barrel.
  • OPS DESKTwo screens, radio stack, map board, antenna feed. The ground station — drone operations run from here.
  • DRONE BAYCharging shelves for small UAS, battery bank, spares wall. Fleet ready.
  • STOVESmall wood stove with a copper flue through the roof, log store, warming bench. The steam-punk heart.
  • AIRLOCKInner door and mudroom: bench, boot rack, drying hooks, grated floor. For cold sites and dirty work.
  • OPEN FLOORNothing fitted. Flex space, a table, or room for a module you have not chosen yet.
MOD.04.3JOIN

JOINJoining and stacking units

Boxes join side by side, end to end, and corner-on-corner. The same four moves that make a courtyard make a pyramid.

  • SINGLE 20'One 20-foot high-cube. Four bays, a door end, a deck. The base one-bedroom cabin or a single work unit.
  • SINGLE 40'One 40-foot high-cube. Eight bays — room for a bedroom, galley, wet room, and a full workshop in one line.
  • TWIN 20'Two 20-foot units side by side with the shared wall opened up: a 4.9 m wide room, eight bays.
  • STACKED LOFTTwo 40-foot units on the ground, a third on top with a roof deck beside it. Sixteen bays down, eight up.
  • ROMAN COURTTwo 40-foot and two 20-foot units around a central courtyard — an atrium with a planted impluvium in the middle. Twenty-four bays.
  • PYRAMID HOUSESeventeen 20-foot units in four stepped tiers, each tier's roof a terraced garden with solar. A ziggurat in the woods.
  • WALLED COMPOUNDA perimeter wall of 40-foot units stacked two high, a four-high turret at each corner, and a central eight-storey tower with an observation deck.
MOD.04.4NOTES

NOTESNotes and caveats

  • Dimensions are standard ISO: 20' = 6.06 × 2.44 m, 40' = 12.19 × 2.44 m, high-cube 2.90 m tall. Bay counts, areas, and the manifest are computed from those figures.
  • Stacking: ISO corner castings and posts carry the loads at sea, so intact boxes stack corner-on-corner. Every opening cut into a wall reduces that; the tall layouts here are design examples, and any real build gets an engineer.
  • Buried / bermed: container walls are not designed for lateral soil load. An earth-sheltered unit needs a retaining structure, membrane waterproofing, and drainage. It is the one option that changes the box.
  • This page is a configurator, not a quotation. Costs, delivery, and site work depend on the ground you are standing on.
